# Solo Business Shield

NIST published a new 33-page small business cybersecurity guide titled "Small Business Cybersecurity: Non-Employer Firms (NIST Cybersecurity White Paper 50)"

It got picked up by Hacker News and the comments were critical of the delivery.

https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50340619

Some HN comments:

"Small businesses don't identify as 'Non-Employer Firms', have no idea what 'NIST' is, most wouldn't know what a White Paper is, nor number 50."

"Since this is intended for small business owners with a thousand competing priorities, hiding the guide between walls of text and down a rabbit hole of unfamiliar links and titles will result in significant drop-off."

"IMO this misses the mark. Yes, it’s a decent attempt at simplifying cybersecurity, but if this is aimed at entry-level, non-technical people, it’s not gonna work."

"i'm technical person and the contents of the document are non-sense academic mumbo jumbo that will not improve anyone's security an iota."

If i were to commission you, a front-end design specialist with an exceptional gift of making the complex seem super simple using modern design aesthetics and user interface designs, how would you design such a web application?

Assume you had the free autonomy to go wild and design from first principles, taking into account your principal target audience, non-tech, super busy, solo-entrepreneurs and the self employed.

I want you to design such a web application choosing a color palette, theme, font, style to suite the desired goal.

Ultimately the final design must meet all the concerns raised in the hacker news thread. The design must remove all complexity such that a florist, beautician, a candle maker, a restaurateur, or a scuba tour operator (busy non techies) are able to navigate their way round in a super intuitive way without feeling overwhelmed.

Consider a Richard Feynman approach of explaining complex ideas simply.

https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/CSWP/NIST.CSWP.50.pdf
